# 4 Evaluation

This chapter reports the paired-plot field evaluation on three pilot farms over two seasons.

## 4.1 Pilot farms and design

The method was evaluated on three pilot farms over two growing seasons. On each farm, two comparable plots of the same crop were irrigated side by side: one by the farmer's usual calendar, one by the adaptive scheduler. Water use was measured with flow meters on each plot's line; yield was weighed at harvest.

## 4.2 Water use

Across the six plot-seasons, the adaptive plots used between 18 % and 31 % less water than the calendar plots. The largest savings came early in the season, when the calendar irrigated a shallow root zone that could not hold the water applied.

## 4.3 Yield

Yield on the adaptive plots was within 4 % of the calendar plots in five of the six plot-seasons, and 9 % lower in one. In that plot-season two of the three sensor nodes failed for eleven days during flowering, and the scheduler fell back to its widened safety margin.

## 4.4 Sensor quality in the field

The quality model excluded at least one node on 23 % of the scheduling days. Most exclusions were caused by radio gaps; a smaller number by step changes after heavy rain cracked the soil around the probe (Okafor et al., 2022).

## 4.5 Threats to validity

Three pilot farms are a small sample, and the plots were chosen by the farmers. The results show that the method can work under field conditions; they do not establish how large the savings would be on a typical farm.
