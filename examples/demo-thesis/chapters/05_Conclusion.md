# 5 Conclusion

This chapter answers the research question and states the limits of the answer.

## 5.1 Answer to the research question

Irrigation can be scheduled from low-cost soil-moisture sensors with less water and without a systematic loss of yield, provided the scheduler treats sensor quality as an input and learns each plot's drainage curve from its own readings. On three pilot farms the adaptive scheduler used between 18 % and 31 % less water than the farmers' calendars, with yield within 4 % in five of six plot-seasons.

## 5.2 Limitations

The evaluation covers one crop, three farms and two seasons. The single plot-season with a 9 % yield loss shows that long sensor outages during flowering remain a risk that the safety margin only partly covers.

## 5.3 Future work

Two directions follow. The first is to share drainage curves between neighbouring plots, so that a plot whose sensors fail can borrow an estimate. The second is to evaluate the method with farmers who operate it without the research team present.
